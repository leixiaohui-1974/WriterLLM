This is my third review of this paper (1st: iteration 1, 6.5/10, Major Revision; 2nd: iteration 4, 8.5/10, Minor Revision). My iteration 4 suggestions have been implemented:

Additionally, the cumulative improvements from all reviewers have produced a comprehensive paper that addresses the DfC-to-DfO paradigm shift from theory (five principles with formal metrics), methodology (seven-layer MBD with software mapping), education (competency framework with Bloom's taxonomy), practice (reference workflow with quantitative MiL results), economics (cost-benefit framing), and adoption (graduated pathway).

From a practitioner perspective, this paper now provides everything a water utility CTO needs to begin the DfO conversation: a clear paradigm definition, computable design metrics, a software tool roadmap, a workforce development plan, a business case, and an adoption strategy. The honest acknowledgment of limitations (single reference case, prototype toolchain, planned but not yet implemented curriculum) builds credibility rather than undermining it.
