## Summary

The paper has improved substantially with water quality integration, stochastic programming comparison, sediment/ice management, operator HMI, and economic analysis. The Miyun case is compelling and the functional decomposition novel. Two issues remain.

## Major Issues

### M1. No climate change discussion
Miyun's catchment faces non-stationary hydrology: observed trends include decreasing baseflow (urbanization) and increasing extreme precipitation (climate change). The 10-year verification period (2014–2023) may not represent future conditions. How robust is the MAS under projected climate scenarios?

### M2. No discussion of multi-reservoir coordination
Miyun does not operate in isolation—it is part of Beijing's water supply system alongside Guanting Reservoir and the SNWD supply. How would the MAS extend to multi-reservoir coordination? This is essential context for the paper's positioning.

## Minor Issues

### m1. Ensemble forecast calibration not discussed
The Xinanjiang + analog matching ensemble (§3.2) has NSE = 0.82 at 24 h. Is the ensemble calibrated (reliable probability bounds)? Rank histograms or CRPS scores would strengthen the forecasting assessment.

### m2. No discussion of dam safety instrumentation
For WSAL-2→3 transition, dam safety monitoring (seepage, deformation, seismicity) should be integrated into the ODD.
